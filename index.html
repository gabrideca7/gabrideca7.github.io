How I work

Four rules, each learned on a real project.

01

No number without a source.

Outsyd once showed star ratings and “−60%” badges that looked great and were invented. I removed every one. A rating, a price or a discount now appears only when it comes from a real source — otherwise the interface shows nothing. Trust is the product.

Seen in Outsyd
02

Treat the client as hostile.

When an app moves real money, every field a phone writes is a possible attack. Outsyd’s cashback is clamped and validated on the server, bookings can only be created as pending, withdrawals run in server-side transactions. That mindset is why my next step is cybersecurity.

Seen in Outsyd
03

Ship, then listen.

Zaptopia went through 36 release builds in three weeks, each one shaped by tester feedback. A bot plays full runs through the real game code, so balance changes get measured instead of guessed.

Seen in Zaptopia
04

AI-assisted, never AI-decided.

I use AI coding agents every day — they make one developer fast. The architecture, the trade-offs, the review and the testing on real devices stay with me, and so does the responsibility for what ships.

About

On the right: graduation day, 2026. In Italy you graduate wearing a laurel wreath.

I’m Gabriele, an independent developer from Altamura — a town on the Murgia plateau in southern Italy, famous mostly for its bread.

Outsyd started from a personal annoyance: arriving in a port city with a few hours and no plan. I built it during my bachelor’s in Computer Science, put it on both app stores and have kept shipping since — features, fixes, release notes, store listings, affiliate partnerships and a TikTok account to bring people in.

Along the way I started building for the people around me: a showcase site for my uncle’s sofa workshop, an online shop for a family dairy and, this autumn, a game of my own.

I’m spending November 2026 in the United States. When I’m back, I start an M.Sc. in Cybersecurity at the University of Pisa.
